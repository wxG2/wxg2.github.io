sEEG-to-Text Cross-modal Decoding

Explore the model ↓Task recording ↓

Align neural representations with language before learning to generate Chinese text from sEEG.

Project overview

Clinical sEEG and written language differ in structure, scale, and variability across subjects. This project explores a two-stage bridge between these modalities rather than treating neural decoding as a fixed-label classification problem.

A CNN–Transformer encoder learns signal representations through brain–text alignment and masked reconstruction. Those representations then condition an autoregressive BART-Chinese decoder, with LoRA rank 8 used for generative fine-tuning.

Reported evaluation: multiple training subjects → one unseen test subject · BLEU-4 0.403

A two-stage signal-to-language framework

Representation pre-training separates two objectives. InfoNCE aligns sEEG hidden states with text hidden states from a frozen BART-Chinese encoder. A masked reconstruction branch provides a complementary signal-learning objective, with reconstruction error measured against the original signal.

Generation fine-tuning reuses the pre-trained sEEG encoder. Its representations condition the BART-Chinese decoder through cross-attention; LoRA rank 8 is used for parameter-efficient adaptation. The frozen label in the diagram applies to the text encoder during pre-training, not to every component in both stages.

Why align before generating?

Cross-modal alignment provides a learning target before language generation. The frozen text encoder acts as a stable language reference, while the sEEG encoder learns to map neural signals toward that representation space.

Masked reconstruction asks the signal branch to recover missing information. Its role is different from InfoNCE — learning structure within the neural modality rather than only matching a paired text representation. Their separate contribution has not been established by a publicly documented ablation.

Cross-attention lets the decoder condition on neural features while retaining an autoregressive language-generation path. LoRA offers a parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism; the available sources do not establish a measured training-memory or runtime improvement for this project.

Reported result — and its scope

The public project summary reports BLEU-4 0.403 when training on multiple subjects and testing on one unseen subject. This evaluates one cross-subject setting; it is not a comparison against other published methods or a claim of state-of-the-art performance.

BLEU-4 measures overlap with reference text. On its own, it does not establish semantic fidelity, subject-independent robustness, or clinical usefulness. Additional held-out subjects, disclosed decoding settings, and aligned prediction–reference examples would make the result easier to assess.

Scope & next steps

The reported BLEU-4 is from a single unseen-subject evaluation, not evidence of general population-level reliability or clinical readiness. Broader held-out-subject testing, fully specified decoding settings, and controlled ablations are next steps rather than completed results.
