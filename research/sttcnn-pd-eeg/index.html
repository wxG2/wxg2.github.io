STTCNN for Parkinson’s EEG Classification

Published as EEG-based Parkinson’s disease classification method by integrating topological features and multi-scale spatio-temporal networks

A topology-aware EEG classifier combining electrode reordering, multiscale spatial features, and period-sensitive temporal modeling for Parkinson’s disease detection.

01

Research problem

EEG combines electrode topology, short-term dynamics, and long-period patterns that single-scale or single-domain models struggle to model jointly.

02

Method

01

Reordered 60 channels according to scalp-electrode positions so adjacent sequence locations better reflect anatomical topology.

02

Used parallel branches: TimesNet captures intraperiod and interperiod temporal variation; progressive convolutions aggregate electrode-, region-, and global-scale spatial features.

03

Combined the two feature streams by element-wise multiplication, then used an LSTM and classifier to distinguish Parkinson’s disease from controls.

03

Technical path

04

My contribution

First author; led problem formulation, model design, experimentation, and manuscript preparation.

05

Results

92.11%PD OFF accuracy
92.00%PD OFF F1
84.08%PD OFF Kappa
  • ↗

    Evaluated on a public auditory Oddball EEG dataset with 24 matched Parkinson’s disease and healthy-control pairs, using five-fold cross-validation.

  • ↗

    For PD OFF versus control, reported 92.11% accuracy, 92.00% F1, 92.54% recall, 91.47% precision, and 84.08% Kappa; PD ON versus control accuracy was 91.20%.

  • ↗

    Ablating the spatiotemporal fusion module reduced accuracy from 92.11% to 81.06% in the PD OFF comparison and from 91.20% to 77.91% in the PD ON comparison.

06

Scope & next steps

These results come from one moderate-sized public cohort and an auditory Oddball protocol. The paper calls for larger, more diverse and multi-center validation, plus testing on other EEG paradigms, before claiming broader clinical utility.
