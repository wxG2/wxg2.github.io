Applied Soft Computing / 2026

EEG-based Parkinson’s disease classification method by integrating topological features and multi-scale spatio-temporal networks

Weixiang Gao, Yunyuan Gao, Jiangwen Lu, Xugang Xi, Xiaohui Lou

Read paper ↗Code ↗

Modeling where EEG signals come from, how they vary across time, and how those features interact.

Abstract

EEG-based Parkinson’s disease classification requires a representation that respects both scalp-electrode topology and temporal dynamics. STTCNN brings these two views together in one model.

Electrodes are reordered to preserve anatomical structure. Parallel branches then capture periodic temporal patterns and spatial features at electrode, regional, and global scales. Their fused representation is modeled with an LSTM for classification.

On a public auditory Oddball EEG cohort, the model achieved 92.11% accuracy for PD OFF versus controls and 91.20% for PD ON versus controls. SHAP analysis examines how different brain regions contribute to its predictions.

24 matched PD–control pairs · 60 EEG channels · five-fold cross-validation

A topology-aware spatiotemporal model

STTCNN first reorganizes the electrode sequence according to scalp topology. It then learns temporal and spatial features in parallel, combines them through element-wise multiplication, and uses an LSTM to model their interaction before classification.

Explore electrode ordering and branch details+

The reordered channels provide an anatomical prior for progressive spatial convolutions. The temporal branch uses TimesNet to capture intraperiod and interperiod variation; the spatial branch aggregates features from electrodes to brain regions and the whole scalp.

Classification performance

The experiments use a public auditory Oddball EEG dataset with 24 matched Parkinson’s disease and healthy-control pairs. PD OFF and PD ON are evaluated separately against controls using five-fold cross-validation.

Accuracy reaches 92.11% for PD OFF and 91.20% for PD ON. The original comparison below reports accuracy and F1 across baselines evaluated under the same conditions.

M0 is the complete model. M1 removes topology rearrangement, M2 the temporal branch, M3 the spatial branch, and M4 the spatiotemporal fusion module. Removing fusion produces the largest accuracy drop in both experiments.

Which brain regions contribute?

SHAP values are aggregated across electrodes within each brain region to examine their contribution to the model’s predictions. The paper uses 100 baseline segments and 32 randomly selected segments for this analysis.

The frontal region has the largest contribution in both experiments. Regional patterns differ between medication conditions, including negative contributions in the PD ON analysis. These maps describe model attribution in this cohort.

Scope & next steps

These results come from one moderate-sized public cohort and an auditory Oddball protocol. The paper calls for larger, more diverse and multi-center validation, plus testing on other EEG paradigms, before claiming broader clinical utility.
