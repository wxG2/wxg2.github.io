01

Problem

Video-level retrieval could not directly support shot-level storytelling, while fully automatic generation offered insufficient control over asset selection, duration, and brand consistency.

02

Challenge

01

Turning a natural-language brief into a controllable, shot-level plan

02

Combining multiple retrieval signals across a large video library

03

Recovering long-running jobs that depend on TTS, external models, object storage, and FFmpeg

04

Managing LLM non-determinism without removing human editorial control

03

Approach

01

Orchestrated requirement parsing, storyboarding, retrieval, confirmation, duration validation, rendering, and delivery through a persistent job state machine.

02

Indexed timestamped video segments with visual descriptions and fused vector, full-text, and tag or keyword retrieval through RRF and business-aware reranking.

03

Added human-in-the-loop checkpoints for full replanning and local shot replacement.

04

Validated duration before rendering and kept long-running stages asynchronous.

06

Key technical decisions

D1

Why hybrid retrieval?

Shot matching needed semantic similarity, exact textual evidence, and structured tags. No single retrieval channel covered all three.

D2

Why RRF?

Reciprocal Rank Fusion combined differently scaled retrieval lists without relying on fragile score calibration.

D3

Why human-in-the-loop?

Editorial choice and brand fit could not be represented by a single automatic score, so the workflow exposed controlled replanning points.

Technical deep dive / GRPO

GRPO for a Structured Editing Planner

Post-trained Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct to produce more reliable structured editing plans while balancing schema, timing, diversity, and narrative constraints.

Approximately 500 training prompts with a seven-dimensional reward design. The public CV names five of the seven reward dimensions; two unspecified dimensions are not inferred here.

R1Schema validity
R2Target duration
R3Valid trim intervals
R4Material diversity
