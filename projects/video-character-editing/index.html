Long-form Video Character Editing

An end-to-end character replacement workflow hardened around segmentation quality, identity locking, abnormal frames, and constrained GPU memory.

01

Problem

Profiles, occlusion, low light, and multi-person scenes caused segmentation or identity failures, while keeping multiple models resident exceeded deployment memory limits.

02

Challenge

01

Mask quality around faces, hair, occlusion, and low-light regions

02

Editing the correct person in multi-person scenes

03

Skipping invalid frames without breaking long-video consistency

04

Coordinating multiple models under limited GPU memory

03

Approach

01

Compared MediaPipe and SegFormer, then replaced the segmentation approach based on mask precision, artifacts, and repaint boundaries.

02

Added target-person detection and locking to avoid editing other people in the frame.

03

Used segmentation-area thresholds, no-face skipping, and dynamic filtering to avoid invalid generation.

04

Developed custom ComfyUI nodes for multi-model orchestration and abnormal-frame handling.

05

Applied on-demand loading, dynamic unloading, and inference quantization to reduce the resource peak.

06

Key technical decisions

D1

Why replace the segmentation path?

The original path was not robust enough for profiles, occlusion, low light, and fine mask boundaries encountered in long videos.

D2

Why lock the target identity?

Multi-person scenes require an explicit identity decision before applying an edit; segmentation alone does not establish who should change.

D3

Why unload models dynamically?

Sequential model stages did not need to remain resident together, so lifecycle control provided a direct way to reduce peak VRAM.
